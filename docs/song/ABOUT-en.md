# "Moongate" — About

## One line

> A song about letting the evening in through the moongate — about someone who has worn a hundred names and fallen a thousand times, and still lands in the same place.

## Short (streaming blurb)

It opens on rain against lantern glass and a harbour turning gold, then throws the night wide when the moongate opens.
The first verse — *"I've worn a hundred names but I keep this one for you"* — turns over by the end into
*"This is my last white feather, and I'm here to stay."*
Three minutes at the temperature of a café afternoon, led by a low D whistle and a Rhodes electric piano.

## Medium

"Moongate" wears the grammar of modern pop on the outside and the grammar of an anime theme underneath.
The chorus sits on the Royal Road progression (IV–V–iii–vi); the last chorus starts stripped of its band and
opens up a whole tone higher. The lyric uses no proper nouns at all — lantern glass, a harbour, a fiddle in the
market, steam off a kettle, feathers on the floor. The pre-chorus states the theme (*"Every ending is a door /
I've walked before"*), the bridge takes it to its lowest point (*"If this is the last life, tell me now —
I'll spend it slower, I'll spend it loud"*), and the final chorus inverts its own hook to close.
A five-note signature on a low D whistle returns three times: the intro, the answers in verse two, and the outro.
